# Environment–Behavior Systems Lab (EBS Lab)

University of Arizona

## About

The Environment–Behavior Systems Lab investigates how human behavior, spatial design, and environmental conditions interact in buildings and cities. The lab uses computational models, simulations, and data analysis to study how people use and adapt to the built environment and how their responses affect environmental performance and human well-being. Drawing on architecture, urban design, and systems engineering, this research informs design and planning for more inclusive and resilient environments.

## Research Areas

- **Human behavior and mobility modeling.** The lab uses agent-based simulation to represent individual decisions and examine how movement and activity patterns vary across people and settings. Research on pedestrian mobility in hot environments considers how street layouts, shade, and heat influence route choice and thermal exposure.
- **Coupled environment–behavior systems.** The lab integrates human behavior into models of building and urban performance. This work examines how spatial layouts and environmental conditions shape the use of buildings and public spaces, and how those patterns of use affect outcomes such as thermal comfort. Digital twins offer a way to link these models with observations from real settings.
- **Performance evaluation and spatial optimization.** The lab develops methods to compare design interventions and assess how well they perform across a range of environmental conditions and behavioral responses. These methods support architectural and planning decisions when future conditions are uncertain.

## Open Source and Repositories

This GitHub space is intended for research code, simulation models, and examples associated with EBS Lab projects. As repositories become available, their documentation will describe how to run the code and cite the associated research.
